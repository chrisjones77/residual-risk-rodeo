# Water Under the Bridge I Can Burn

Role: unsequenced workshop candidate; possible late-album resolution track  
Narrator: reflective, dry, tired, and increasingly certain  
Emotional position: after anger, before peace  
Central turn: perseverance stops being a virtue when it preserves a route the narrator no longer needs

## Core thesis

The past has passed, and the narrator no longer needs a way back. Burning the bridge means foreclosing his own return, not harming anyone or anything on the opposite bank. He is not seeking vindication, explanation, revenge, acknowledgment, or a verdict.

The song should remain open enough to describe a failed relationship, a friendship ending, departure from a hometown or career, grief over an institution, or the recognition that someone simply stayed too long.

## North star

The title may sound vaguely threatening before the song begins. By the final line, it must mean the opposite: the water is gone, the bridge served its purpose, and the narrator peacefully accepts that he does not need a route back.

## Musical identity

- Northern Ontario progressive bluegrass / country-gothic ballad
- 79 BPM, subtle 4/4 two-step
- D minor or E minor
- Mature, weathered male baritone
- Dry close-miked acoustic guitar and simple upright bass
- Sparse melodic banjo, withheld until the first chorus
- Mournful fiddle as the primary emotional instrument
- Restrained sustained dobro answers
- Brushes and rim clicks expanding modestly in the choruses
- Three-part male harmony on "water under the bridge"; primarily solo on "the bridge I can burn"
- Quiet opening, moderate choruses, stripped bridge, broad but peaceful final chorus, quiet ending
- Organic production, slight analog warmth, and a wide natural acoustic soundstage

## Narrative architecture

1. **The river:** acoustic guitar, distant fiddle harmonic, and nearly subliminal river ambience.
2. **Stayed too long:** maintenance imagery reveals that the narrator treated structural failure as one more repair.
3. **Recognition:** the river has already moved on.
4. **What remains:** three boxes and a few ambiguous remnants wait on the other shore.
5. **Hindsight:** exits, warning lights, roads not taken, and signs repaired instead of read.
6. **Instrumental resolution:** eight bars of fiddle, four of banjo, then four intertwined.
7. **No verdict required:** the arrangement strips back while the narrator releases the need to be right.
8. **Peace:** the final chorus expands without triumph, then recedes to one fiddle note.

## Guardrails

- The narrator closes only his own route backward.
- Keep imagery within river, bridge, current, timber, matches, smoke, opposite bank, and road home.
- Use rural and geographic specificity, not corporate or legal detail.
- No revenge, threats, regret directed at others, verdict language, confidential detail, identifiable chronology, righteous-victim framing, or declarations of winning.
- Avoid references to employers, lawyers, boards, medical leave, technology, shares, police, settlements, and litigation.
- Treat the old bridge as familiar and once valuable, not inherently evil.
