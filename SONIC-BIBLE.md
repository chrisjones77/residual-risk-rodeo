# Sonic Bible

## Centre of gravity

Every track must retain at least one audible roots anchor: banjo roll, fiddle phrase, upright bass pulse, dobro, flat-picked guitar, group harmony, or square-dance cadence.

## Expansion palette

- Blast beats and double-kick percussion for escalation, not decoration
- Industrial percussion built from believable worksite textures
- Gospel harmony when the committee becomes a community
- Drones for weather, river, memory, and unresolved conditions
- Spoken announcements used sparingly as structural markers
- Silence or reduced instrumentation after significant lyrical turns

## Vocal world

Use weathered, plainspoken lead voices rather than theatrical caricatures. Group vocals represent the committee or community. Spoken passages should sound like a real person reading a document they have begun to doubt.

## Writing rules

- Use concrete nouns: gravel, guard, permit, cribbing, rain, radio, bridge, chain, coffee.
- Let procedural language reveal emotion indirectly.
- Give every comic song one honest line.
- Give every serious song one moment of dry wit.
- Avoid generic rebellion, outlaw-country poses, and empty corporate satire.
- Do not explain the entire Wasi River universe in any single song.

## Recurring musical signatures

- A three-note banjo warning figure
- A clipped radio tone before major transitions
- The phrase "river still running" in tracks 1, 10, and 12
- A final unresolved fiddle note that carries from track 11 into track 12
